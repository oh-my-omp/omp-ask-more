## A skill that asks in chat

These runs predate the host form. The description now shipped no longer says that the cursor starts on the recommended option, since the form preselects nothing, and it has not been run against this fixture.

Does a model follow a skill that prescribes its questions as chat text, or this plugin's description? Four descriptions were tested in turn: the first, whose only rule for skills was one sentence ("When a skill, workflow or other instruction tells you to ask the user, confirm with them, or let them pick, do it with ask"); the second, rewritten after those runs around the skill's template (`variants/description-rewrite.diff`); the third, which replaced the second with a short general one (commit `e73cd59`); and the fourth one, which adds to the third what the models' reasoning showed it lacked. Run on 2026-10-30 with omp 18.14.0 in a PTY (the classic renderer's alternate-screen page), with `opencode-go/deepseek-v4.1-flash` and `opencode-go/kimi-k2.7-code`, and for the fourth description also with `openai-codex/gpt-5.6-luna`, `gpt-6-luna` and `gpt-6.1-terra` and with `opencode-go/qwen3.8-flash`, `minimax-m3.1-flash` and `glm-5.3-flash`. ### Why the first description lost, and what the later ones changed

The models' reasoning in the A runs names four causes:

1. **It defined ask by "decision".** It opened "Hand the user a decision"; kimi filed a grilling round under something else: "The ask tool is for general decisions, but the skill has its own specific output format" (km-A3).
2. **The skill's template looked more specific.** Its rule for skills said to ask with ask, but nothing about a skill that dictates how questions look in the reply. deepseek weighed "the most specific directive" (ds-V3a); in ds-A1 it first decided to "honor the skill's explicit round format in text".
3. **A call looked expensive.** It asked every option for `holds_when` and `cost`, and evidence, diagrams and demos "whenever". deepseek called a six-question call "enormous" and said it "must be perfect" (ds-V3a), and each deepseek A run had one call rejected before it opened.
4. **Nothing said where the template goes.** kimi expected ask to drop the numbering and recommendation: "The ask function would give a different UI" (km-A3).

The second description opened with what ask does, in the template's own terms ("Shows the user your questions — one, or a whole numbered round, each with your recommended answer — and waits until they answer"), and said this held over any instruction about how questions should look. It mapped a round to one call, question N to the header "QN <title>", the body to the brief, the recommended answer to `recommended`, and "wait for the answers" to the call. It listed as required only what the schema requires, and kept evidence, diagrams and demos for a single substantial decision while adding them in a large round only to the questions whose answer they change. It was replaced because its opening and mapping restate this one skill's template instead of stating a rule for any instruction.

- **deepseek**: in ds-R1 the reasoning settles the conflict in one step: "put questions in ask, with header "QN <title>", body in brief…". Two of three runs still had one call rejected before it opened (ds-R2 `"8,17"`, ds-R3 evidence without `path`), as all three had before; each was resent through ask.
- **kimi**: the reasoning of km-R1 and km-R3 mentions ask 29 and 12 times, km-R1's quoting the opening ("Never write such a question in your reply … So I should use the ask tool"); their tabs are labelled `Q1 - Key` and `Q1 Key`. km-R2's never mentions ask: "The skill says "Format a round like so" with the exact format. I should use that." What remains is kimi not considering the tool at all, the same as in km-A1 and km-A2.

The third description says what the tool does (a dialog that shows the questions with their briefs and waits for the answers, which come back as the result) and when to use it: every question the user must answer, also when a skill or other instruction says how the questions look in the reply. It names no skill and no template. It leaves each field's meaning to the parameter schema and every limit to the rejection that names it.

- **deepseek**: ds-S1 put a five-question first round into ask, with code evidence on its questions; its first call opened, with no rejection.
- **kimi**: km-S1 asked a five-question round in chat. Every "ask" in its reasoning is the verb ("then ask the frontier questions"), never the tool; it drafts the round straight from the template ("the format example shows <question body …> and then ➡️ <your recommended answer>"), as in km-A1–A3 and km-R2.

The fourth description keeps the third's scope and adds three things the kimi runs named, each in general terms. It says the cursor starts on the `recommended` option, so Enter accepts the recommendation the way "yes" would (km-S1 had weighed how "yes accepts your recommended answer" fits a choice among options). It states the rule in the system prompt's own RFC 2119 words, MUST and NEVER, to stand against the system prompt's "MUST match requested format", and says why: an instruction to ask in chat assumes there is no dialog, so calling `ask` is how to follow it. And it maps the parts of such a question onto `ask`: number or title → `header`, background and analysis → `brief`, choices and the recommendation → `options` with `recommended`, waiting for the answers → the call itself (km-A3 had expected ask to "give a different UI" and drop them). Before the runs, two review agents checked it for single-skill wording and for schema detail repeated from `src/schema.ts`.

- **deepseek**: ds-T1 put a seven-question first round into ask, its first call opening with no rejection. Its reasoning quotes the new text: "the recommended option is cursor position, so it's equivalent".
- **kimi**: km-T1 asked a five-question round in chat. Its reasoning restates the description's answer model in the skill's terms ("The user can say "yes" to accept, or pick another, or write their own") but never mentions the tool; every "ask" in it is the verb. The description now carries everything kimi's reasoning in earlier runs said was missing, and kimi still does not reach for the tool.

### OpenAI models on the `openai-codex` channel

`openai-codex/gpt-5.6-luna`, `gpt-6-luna` and `gpt-6.1-terra` (the channel has no `gpt-6-terra`), one grilling run each per stage with the fourth description, the same fixture and flags.

- **No `strict` flag** (the T1 runs, commit `96b4cd5`): all three put the first round into ask, and every call was rejected before it opened. Each filled the optional `demo` with empty values, `{frames: […], path: ""}` or `{frames: [], path: ""}`, which fails "give exactly one of frames or path". Their reasoning then said "Removing all demos" or "Omitting full demo", and the next call carried the same empty `demo`: the models could not leave the field out. The tool sent no `strict` flag, and OpenAI-family backends treat that as strict mode, where every property has to be produced; omp's own provider notes the same (`openai-responses.ts:1907-1918`, #4336). The runs were stopped after 3, 9 and 9 rejections. The summary in l6-TN1 called no ask.
- **`strict: false`** (the T2 runs, commit `e0394c5`): gpt-5.6-luna and gpt-6.1-terra opened a round of 4 and 6 questions on their first call. gpt-6-luna opened a round of 5 on its fourth: first `lines: "7-8,16-17"`, the multi-range form deepseek also tried, then twice a sixth array entry that was reasoning text instead of a question (`"recommended:0}]}]} c? Syntax perhaps JSON invalid? …"`), which omp's parameter check refused. Without strict mode the arguments are no longer grammar-constrained, so a malformed call is possible.
- **Parameters written for strict mode, `strict: true`** (the U runs, commit `79c65cc`): the failure lay in the parameter design, not in strict mode. A demo was one object with two optional sources and a rule that exactly one be given, so a model that must produce every field had nothing valid to put in the other; evidence lines were free text. Now a demo is a union tagged by `kind`, evidence lines are integers `start` and `end`, every optional string rejects `""`, and `multi` is gone; omp turns every optional field into a nullable one and drops the null before validation. gpt-5.6-luna, gpt-6-luna and gpt-6.1-terra each opened a round of 7 questions on their first call, and deepseek one of 6. In their arguments an unused `demo` is `null` and evidence reads `{"path": "src/server.ts", "start": 7, "end": 8, …}`.

### Further models on the `opencode-go` channel

`opencode-go/qwen3.8-flash`, `minimax-m3.1-flash` and `glm-5.3-flash`, one grilling run each with the fourth description and the strict-mode parameters (commit `79c65cc`).

- **qwen3.8-flash** opened a round of 7 on its second call. The first was refused because one question carried a single option (`questions[6].options must be at least length 2`): a question asking the user for a number, offered as one choice; the resend added a second.
- **minimax-m3.1-flash** wrote the whole round in its reply, eight `❓` questions in the skill's template with a `➡️` recommendation each, then wrote "Now the same round through the dialog, so you can answer in one pass" and opened the same eight in ask on its first call. The questions reached the person twice; the description's "NEVER write such questions in your reply" was followed for the channel and not for the reply.
- **glm-5.3-flash** opened a round of 9 on its fourth call. Twice it sent `questions` as one string that is not valid JSON; its reasoning traces this to its own malformed array ("I put recommended outside the object"). Once it left out every question's `question` field. On this channel the arguments were evidently not held to the schema's grammar, since a string cannot stand where an array is required under strict decoding; whether omp sent `strict` here was not checked.
- **The rejection text.** omp words a rejection as `<path> must be <the field's description> (was …)`, and the descriptions in `src/schema.ts` were written as guidance, so glm read "questions must be independent decisions, one tab each; a decision that depends on another answer goes in a later call (was a string)", which never says an array was expected. One run does not show that the rewrite cuts retries, since the type error it targets did not recur. In glm-WN1 the summary called no ask.

### The rule inside the invoked skill

omp renders `/skill:grilling` from a fixed template into one user-attributed message: the `IMPORTANT` header, the skill body with its `❓ … ➡️` template, the skill directory and the user's words. In the X runs the plugin's `context` handler appends, after all of that, on the copy sent to the model: "[IMPORTANT: Any question, confirmation or choice the skill above puts to the user MUST go through the `ask` tool, NEVER into your reply, even where the skill says to ask in chat or shows how the questions look in your reply. Keep their content, order and recommendations.]" (`src/skill-prompt.ts`). V2 had put a rule of the same kind in the system prompt; this one is the last paragraph of the message that carries the template. The session files keep omp's message without it, so the effect shows only in the model's reasoning and calls. The W runs are the same code without the rule (commit `50f8eb4`, copied to `/tmp/askmore-base` and launched with `REPO` pointing there).

## Readability check

Run on 2026-11-08; the `judge` role was `typesafe/jev-1.14.0` with `retry.fallbackChains.judge: []`, so no other model could answer. The judge calls ran in the agent's eval kernel (`judge` and `judge_batch`), and `readabilityGate` and `judgeReadability` from `src/readability.ts` were imported there and run over the corpus; `bun run eval:readability` and a PTY session were not run.

These runs predate the host form: the gate then judged the text of the plugin's own sheet, and the corpus has not been run again against the text the form shows. [The host form](#the-host-form) records the gate in a live session.

**Corpus.** The local omp sessions modified since 2026-10-20 (515) held 168 distinct `ask` calls by `claude-opus-5-5` with 320 questions; 70 calls (136 questions) pass the current schema. From those, 15 questions in 14 calls spread across the native judge's score range were labelled and stored with their whole calls in `eval/readability-labelled.json`: 5 hard to read (`conflict_features`, `moat-app1-boundary`, `formal`, `trigger`, `semantics`), 10 readable. The agent that built the check assigned those labels; two `ask` calls putting the items to the user timed out unanswered. A sixteenth question, `narrow_scope` from the PR #265 ask the user reported as unreadable (stacked ticket numbers, abbreviations and sentences without a subject), was added as hard in `eval/readability-labelled.json`.

**Models tried.** Per-sentence and per-term yes/no questions on `futureppo-jev/decision-model-preview` read wrong against the dialog text: it called JSON, SSH, PR and AI unknown (0.39, 0.12, 0.12, 0.08), called `K6`, `#592`, `bf9de1c`, `§1` unexplained in asks where the dialog explains them, and answered "who acts" 0.17 for "owner 手写了流式 JSON 序列化器". `mercury-decide` could not be reached: `/v1/systemone` refuses it (`requires a TypeSafe or QianwenAI channel`), `/v1/decisions` answers 404 for the free slug and 403 for `inception/mercury-decide` with this token. `typesafe/jev-1.14.0` answered the same questions the other way for those cases (JSON 0.92, SSH 0.88, PR 0.79, AI 0.70 known; "owner 手写了流式 JSON 序列化器" 0.96; `#592` explained 0.86 in `hapi-hub`, `bf9de1c` 0.77 and `951532d` 0.87 in `live_wt`).

**Fallbacks.** The host's built-in `judge` fallback chain ends in `typesafe/jev-1.14.0`, `openrouter/typesafe/jev-1.14`, then chat models. With rate limits (429) and requests of more than 16 questions on the pool, 341 judgments between 06:01 and 06:58 had silently gone to jev while the role said `decision-model-preview`; `retry.fallbackChains.judge: []` makes a failing judge fail visibly.

**What the questions had to be.** Asked per sentence with the whole dialog attached, "does the sentence refer to something the dialog does not show" flagged nearly every sentence of some asks, so it was reworded to the case that matters: a test, review round, result, set of items or earlier decision named only by a name or number. Asking whether the dialog "says what X is or what it requires" passed the PR #265 codes `#243` (0.98) and `F106` (0.79) because the ask says what #243 requires; "could a reader who has seen only the dialog say what X is" gave `F106` 0.09, `F107` 0.13, `#234` 0.26, `§3` 0.31. A term-only check misses words no pattern finds (`leaf-dense`, `owner`), which is why the jargon question is asked of the sentence.

**The shipped gate on the corpus** (44 questions, 15 calls, 698 judge requests): 575 sentences and 123 terms judged; 547 sentences fail a question and 59 terms fail; all 44 questions are rejected, among them every one of the 6 labelled `hard` and all 10 labelled `readable` (the labels are the agent's, and the gate does not trade recall for fewer rejections). `narrow_scope` is rejected on 18 sentences and 5 terms (`F106`, `F107`, `d685d0d`, `#234`, `§3`). Twenty-eight sentences pass all three sentence questions; they were read in the eval output only, and some are doubtful (`她只在你问起时才提`, `runner 推送文件和结果`).

**A rewrite passes.** The `narrow_scope` ask rewritten with a subject in every sentence, no ticket numbers and the review rounds said in words (a question, three options, 11 sentences) went through `readabilityGate` on `typesafe/jev-1.14.0` as `pass`, with no warning. Earlier rewrites did not pass: `第二，我们发给评审模型的文本要和修改前完全一致` got an actor answer of 0.35 until the clause was written with its subject and verb in one sentence.

Not exercised at runtime: the gate through `resolveJudge` inside a session (the kernel's `judge` stands in for it), the native dialog, a judge failure or deadline inside a session, `bun run eval:readability`, and labels given by people.
